From Biot-Savart to Circular Fields: Student Coordination of Magnetic Field Representations in AR
Michele W. McColgan, George E. Hassel, Kamyar Pashayi, and Jason W. Morphew · PERC 2026

Students often struggle to coordinate right-hand rules, vector equations, and spatial representations when reasoning about magnetic fields. Using the Mathematical Sensemaking framework, this study examines two contrasting student cases involving the long-wire and Biot–Savart MARVLS models. Both students reasoned productively within individual representations, but they differed in whether they used the AR environment to test and coordinate the local Biot–Savart field direction with the global circular magnetic-field model.

Making Magnetism Visible: An NSF IUSE Project Investigating Student Sensemaking with MARVLS Augmented Reality Apps
Michele W. McColgan, George E. Hassel, Kamyar Pashayi, and Jason W. Morphew · ASEE 2026

The MARVLS AR Apps scaffold students’ ability to visualize abstract three-dimensional magnetism concepts and coordinate mathematical expressions with physical representations. The project developed and implemented AR modules in introductory physics courses over three years and analyzed pre/post conceptual assessments and semi-structured interviews. Findings indicate improved magnetism conceptual understanding and qualitative evidence that AR can support coordination between mathematical and physical reasoning modes.

Seeing Electromagnetic Induction: Student Sense Making with Augmented Reality
Michele W. McColgan, George E. Hassel, Kamyar Pashayi, and Jason W. Morphew · ASEE 2026

This study maps students’ conceptualization of magnetic flux and electromagnetic induction while they manipulate MARVLS AR visualizations. Using the Mathematical Sensemaking framework and think-aloud interviews, the analysis examines how students use a copper ring, magnetic-field representation, gesture, and model manipulation to reason about changing flux and the resulting direction of induced current.

MSM Framework: Augmented Reality Models of 3D Vectors
Michele McColgan, George E. Hassel, Kamyar Pashayi, Jason Morphew, and Junior Anthony Bennett · ASEE 2025

This study investigates augmented reality as a tool for strengthening mathematical understanding and conceptualization in physics and engineering. It uses the Mathematical Sensemaking framework to examine student reasoning about vectors, coordinate systems, and magnetic fields while students interact with manipulable 3D MARVLS models. The study reports evidence that AR can support spatial reasoning and movement between mathematical and physical reasoning.

MSM Framework: AR Model of the Force on a Charge Moving in a Magnetic Field
Michele W. McColgan, George E. Hassel, and Kamyar Pashayi · PERC 2024

This case study uses MARVLS to investigate student understanding of the direction of magnetic force on a moving charged particle. The Mathematical Sensemaking framework is used to map reasoning across diagrams, concepts, symbols, equations, and right-hand-rule gestures. Interview results suggest that MARVLS tools and Merge Cube interaction can support deeper understanding of the Lorentz-force cross product.

Understanding Magnetism Concepts Through Augmented Reality: A Qualitative Analysis
Michele W. McColgan, Jason Morphew, George E. Hassel, Junior Anthony Bennett, and Megan Clark Kelly · ASEE 2024

This qualitative study examines how students develop conceptual understanding while interacting with a MARVLS model of the magnetic field surrounding a long current-carrying wire. Semi-structured interviews and guided interactions are used to investigate how students incorporate manipulable AR visualizations into their understanding of magnetism and coordinate 3D models with conventional representations.

Embodied Learning with Gesture Representation in an Immersive Technology Environment in STEM Education
Junior A. Bennett, Jason W. Morphew, and Michele W. McColgan · ASEE 2024

This multiple-case study examines student gestures during interactions with augmented-reality visualizations. Preliminary findings indicate that students use gestures that represent AR visualizations to think and reason about abstract concepts such as electric and magnetic fields, and that these gestures may relate to mental simulation during problem solving.

Augmented Reality to Scaffold 2D Representations of 3D Models in Magnetism
Michele W. McColgan, George E. Hassel, Natalie C. Stagnitti, Jason W. Morphew, and Rebecca Lindell · PERC 2023

This paper reports the initial implementation of MARVLS in three introductory physics sections at Siena. The activities emphasize relationships between two-dimensional and three-dimensional representations of magnetism and use manipulable AR models displayed on Merge Cubes. The study compares pre/post assessment data and student feedback, reporting modest gains in electromagnetism concepts and spatial reasoning in the calculus-based sections.

Electricity & Magnetism Conceptual Assessment

Assessing Students’ Conceptual Knowledge of Electricity and Magnetism
Michele W. McColgan, Rose A. Finn, Darren L. Broder, and George E. Hassel · Physical Review Physics Education Research, 2017

This paper presents the Electricity and Magnetism Conceptual Assessment (EMCA), an assessment aligned with second-semester introductory physics. It covers electrostatics, electric fields, circuits, magnetism, and induction and reports development, validation, and performance data from students at Siena and George Washington University.
